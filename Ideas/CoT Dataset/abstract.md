
We introduce **Mind’s Eye**, a large-scale benchmark and evaluation framework designed to probe the visuocognitive reasoning capabilities of vision–language models (VLMs) through psychometrically grounded tasks. Mind’s Eye systematically isolates core cognitive transformations organized into a three-tier taxonomy inspired by Carroll’s theory of fluid intelligence. Our design controls for perceptual confounds by embedding both **questions and candidate options within the same image**, forcing models to **disambiguate reference regions from distractor options**, a setting where state-of-the-art VLMs consistently underperform. We complement this with a multi-prompt evaluation protocol (meta-task framing, step-wise reasoning, and concept-tag hints) to expose sensitivity to prompt scaffolding. Empirically, we evaluate open- and closed-source VLMs (Qwen-VL, LLaVA, Idefics, GPT-4V, Gemini) under this unified setup, revealing sharp performance gaps: while humans achieve near-ceiling accuracy, leading VLMs fail to generalize across tasks, often misallocating attention to option regions. Together, our contributions establish _Mind’s Eye_ as a rigorous testbed for multimodal fluid intelligence and highlight the need for **geometry-aware training and evaluation** of VLMs.

Title: Mind's Eye: A Benchmark of Visual Abstraction, Transformation and Composition for Multimodal LLMs. 

Abstract:
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have recently shown remarkable progress on vision--language benchmarks, yet their capacity for cognitive and psychological reasoning remains underexplored. We introduce Mind’s Eye, a benchmark of eight visuocognitive tasks inspired by classic human intelligence tests, organized under a novel A-R-T taxonomy: Abstraction, Relation, and Transformation. These tasks probe core processes of fluid intelligence such as pattern induction, analogical mapping, and mental manipulation of shapes. Through large-scale evaluation of both open- and closed-source MLLMs, and comparison with non-expert human participants, we uncover stark performance gaps: models consistently underperform humans despite the latter having no domain training. Our analysis reveals that MLLMs fundamentally lack cognitive mechanisms required for visuospatial reasoning, exhibiting (i) misallocated visual attention, (ii) unstable internal manipulation of percepts, (iii) over-reliance on domain knowledge rather than abstract concept learning, and (iv) systematic failures to disambiguate visual questions from answer options within the same image. Together, these findings provide evidence that current MLLMs do not yet approximate fluid reasoning ability, and motivate the design of cognitively grounded benchmarks such as  Mind’s Eye to chart progress toward general multimodal intelligence.
