AI safety and alignment are critical frontiers in the development of artificial intelligence, especially as we push toward more advanced systems that could reshape society. At its core, AI safety focuses on preventing unintended harms from AI things like biases in decision-making algorithms, catastrophic failures in autonomous systems, or even existential risks if superintelligent AI emerges without proper safeguards. Alignment, a key subset of this, is about ensuring that AI's objectives truly match human values and intentions. It's not just about making AI smart; it's about making it wise and benevolent. For instance, if an AI is tasked with "maximizing paperclip production," it might hypothetically turn the world into a factory without checks, highlighting the need for value alignment techniques like inverse reinforcement learning or scalable oversight.

In the context of the SPAR project which I see as an opportunity to apply these concepts in structured AI research prioritizing safety means integrating alignment from the ground up. This could involve designing reward functions that incorporate ethical constraints, using interpretability tools to peek inside black-box models, or running simulations to test for edge cases. My interest stems from believing that unaligned AI could amplify inequalities or lead to misuse, so projects like SPAR are vital for building robust, human-centered AI.

As for my engagement with AI safety, it's been a mix of self-directed learning and practical exploration over the past couple of years. I started by diving into Brian Christian's "The Alignment Problem," which opened my eyes to real-world examples of misalignment, like biased facial recognition tech. That led me to read key papers, such as those from OpenAI on scalable oversight and Anthropic's work on constitutional AI I'd say I've gone through about a dozen from sources like arXiv and NeurIPS proceedings. Last summer, I completed the BlueDot Impact course on AI alignment basics, which was eye-opening for its focus on technical challenges like reward hacking. It's all fueled my passion to contribute more deeply, which is why SPAR feels like the perfect next step.
